03 · The Creative Core

A five-label semantic taxonomy — not a binary conflict / gap check.

The AI's job is not to declare right or wrong. It characterises the relationship precisely enough that a human drafter can decide what to do with it. The taxonomy came from a working drafter, not a generic NLP template.

aligns-with
The draft matches the reference position.
differs-on
A deliberate divergence — carries a tighten / loosen / neutral sentiment.
conflicts-with
A genuine inconsistency that needs resolving.
silent-on
The reference covers ground the draft leaves unaddressed.
goes-beyond
The draft exceeds the reference standard.

BNM often deliberately diverges from an international standard — a finder that can only say "conflict" would flag intentional policy choices as errors.

03 · Innovation & Creativity

The prototype demonstrates a pattern that generalises.

Characterise, don't judge

A five-label taxonomy captures what a binary check cannot — that a divergence can be an intentional, well-reasoned policy choice, not an error to be corrected.

Cross-workstream linkage, first-class

One engine runs across two independent, structurally different workstreams. Linkage between them is a first-class finding — not a per-document afterthought.

Recommendations with teeth

Agents propose clause-cited, labelled, reviewable recommendations — while the non-negotiable rule "never invent a citation" is enforced in code, outside the model entirely.

Any two regulatory artefacts, from any department, become instantly comparable — without new engine code, only a new corpus.

04 · Technical Execution

Specialised agents over a single general-purpose model.

Rather than asking one LLM to "compare these two documents," the engine runs a six-stage finder→critic pipeline across three model tiers. Cost and latency scale with task difficulty — not by running the most expensive model on every token.

Traceable

Each finding is accepted or dismissed by hand. Drafting then happens with that context — and nothing is signed off by the person who proposed it.

Assisted

An assistant answers questions with the same clause-cited discipline — turning accepted findings into concrete policy positions during drafting.

SELARASFeasibility
07 · Scalability

New policy domains arrive by data expansion, not software redevelopment.

Right-sized model per step

Haiku extracts, Sonnet judges, Opus covers the one step that needs it. Cost scales with the cheap stages, not the expensive one.

Retrieval, not brute force

The pipeline filters to ~60 candidate pairs per document pair — cost grows with candidates, not with the square of document size.

Caching + a hard ceiling

Topic axes are cached per document (re-analysis is near-free), and a per-document cap means no single click can fan out unboundedly.

Adding a workstream is data, not code

Each workstream is a self-contained folder — graph, anchors, axes, findings. 5 sit side by side with zero per-workstream code.

Batching

The finder judges 8 candidate pairs per model call, not 1 — an 8× reduction on the dominant call volume.

Regression safety at scale

1,195 tests run in CI on every PR — including guards that stop retired vocabulary or a shrunken clause index from creeping back.

09 · Risk & Mitigations

The failure modes are designed out, in code.

RISK 01

The AI gets it wrong

A finding cites a clause that doesn't exist, or misjudges one that does.

Mitigation: Citations are validated in code, not by the model. The drafter accepts or rejects every finding.

RISK 02

Confidentiality

Bank drafts or internal papers leave the Bank.

Mitigation: Prototype built on public documents, entirely on the Bank's infrastructure. Private model deployment, not a consumer chatbot.

RISK 03

Accountability

A policy ships with an error and nobody owns the decision.

Mitigation: Maker-checker (Phase 2) with a full audit trail. A checker cannot approve their own work — enforced in code.
